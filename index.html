28 DAYS

Classroom Edition

A free game that teaches the menstrual cycle to middle school health classes. Students play through a first period and the month after it, with a lesson and a quiz every school day.

Play the game Teacher guide

Runs in a web browser. Nothing to install, no accounts, no student information collected. Needs a keyboard.

A self-playing preview of the game, about two minutes long.

WHAT STUDENTS LEARN

The whole cycle, by living through one

Students play Ruby, a middle schooler whose first period starts in the middle of health class. They pack her bag, sit through Dr. Flo's lessons, deal with leaks and cramps, and log each day in a cycle tracker. Three boys in the story ask questions and learn alongside her, so it works for the whole class.

What a period is

Why it happens, how long it lasts, and what counts as normal.

The four phases

Menstrual, follicular, ovulation and luteal, and the hormone behind each.

Products

Pads, liners, tampons, cups and period underwear, and how often to change each.

Cramps and PMS

What causes them and what helps.

Tracking

How logging a cycle helps you predict the next period.

When to get help

The signs that mean it's time to talk to a doctor, and who a trusted adult is.

IN YOUR CLASS

Built to fit a class period

The game is split into eight in-game days. "Choose a day" on the title screen starts a class on any of them, with supplies already stocked.

  • A printable teacher guide with pacing plans
  • A discussion question for every day
  • An answer key for all 23 quiz questions
  • A one-page student worksheet with answers
  • A glossary with a labeled body diagram
  • Sound starts off
Open the teacher guide

What it covers

The biology of the menstrual cycle, period products and hygiene, cramps, PMS, mood changes, cycle tracking, and when to see a doctor.

What it doesn't

Sexual activity, contraception, or pregnancy. Pregnancy is mentioned only to explain why the lining of the uterus builds up each cycle.

Please have a school nurse or health teacher review it before using it with students. It has not yet been reviewed by a medical professional.

THE FACTS

Every fact shows where it came from

The game has 31 "Did you know?" facts, five health lessons and a 28-word glossary. Each was checked against pages from the CDC, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), the NIH and a few others. Where sources disagree, the game uses ACOG's number.

The full list of claims and sources is public: read the fact check.

Privacy. No names, no logins, no cookies. Progress is saved only in the browser on that computer. The site counts visits anonymously so its author can see how often it is used.

28 Days is not medical advice. Students with questions about their own bodies should talk to a parent or guardian, a school nurse, or a doctor.
